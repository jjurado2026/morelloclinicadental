**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the

In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on

## 1. The idea
A calm page where the pictures are placed like prints on a gallery wall: each at its own size and position, with text set small and light between them. Nothing lines up in the obvious way, yet everything sits on a grid you can feel. The unevenness is what makes it read as art-directed by a person: a small portrait far left, a wide landscape running off the right edge, a square that overlaps the next section. It lets photographs lead without ever becoming a slideshow.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A 12-column grid with wide margins (5–7vw). The hero is a single line of large light type (6–9vw) across the top third, and below it one photograph placed off-centre (columns 6–12, 16:10), its top edge overlapping the baseline zone of the headline by a few pixels of deliberate tension. Each following section places two or three photographs with different spans and vertical offsets: for example a 3:4 portrait in columns 2–5 starting 120px lower than a 16:10 landscape in columns 7–12; then a single small 1:1 in columns 9–11 beside a paragraph in columns 2–6. Paragraphs are short (max 40ch) and sit in the gaps. No two consecutive sections use the same arrangement. Vertical spacing is large and irregular on purpose (120–260px).

## 3. Type — Switzer
A single roman weight of a Swiss neo-grotesk at two or three modest sizes; position and grey value do the work that size and bold usually do. Suits architects, galleries, studios and any quiet page where photographs carry the scale.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=switzer@400,500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Switzer** — weight 400 · tracking -0.03em · leading 1.1 · sentence case; headings stay small (28–40px) and at most one line jumps to 5vw
- Text: **Switzer** — 400 for everything, 500 only for names and links · leading 1.45 · tracking -0.01em; hierarchy by ink against mid-grey
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
A light or regular weight at large size for the few headings; small, quiet text. Serif or a slender sans. Everything left-aligned to grid lines.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 6. Hero — Three prints, one line
Photographs lead and the type stays small. A pale ground, a 12-column grid, 5vw side margins. The top row at 15px holds the wordmark left, three text links centred and the single action right. Three photographs with square corners hang from one shared top line at 18% of the viewport height, like prints pinned along a rail, each a different size so their bottom edges fall at three heights. A small 1:1 in columns 1-2 (about 190px). A tall 3:4 in columns 4-7, about 440px wide and 590px tall, the largest thing on screen. A 16:10 landscape in columns 9-12 that continues past the right margin and is cropped by the viewport edge. Columns 3 and 8 are left empty as breathing space. The headline sits under the small photograph, left-aligned to its left edge across columns 1-3: a light serif or slender sans at clamp(30px, 2.8vw, 44px) on two lines, followed by one sentence at 16px in the soft ink, max 30ch. One line of caption at 14px sits under the left corner of each of the other two photographs.

On a phone: The rail turns vertical, so left edges vary instead of bottoms. The tall photograph comes first at 72% of the width on the left margin; the small square follows at 38% of the width, pushed to the right margin and overlapping the first by 40px; the landscape runs full bleed below. The headline at 8vw sits beside the square on the left, with the sentence at 16px under it.
Headline: A quiet 3-6 word line at about 40px, at most 36 characters across two lines.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Switzer, text Switzer) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.

## 7. Sections, in order

### 1. intro — Heading captions a panorama (full-bleed)
The section is exactly 100svh. Its top third holds a two-line heading of three to six words at clamp(48px, 6.5vw, 96px) with leading 0.95, either centred or flush left on the page margin. 28px beneath the heading sit two small captions side by side, each two lines at 13px and 150px wide with a 48px gap, aligned to the heading (centred as a pair, or both starting at its left edge). 40px below them a full-bleed photograph begins and runs off the bottom of the viewport: it is 100vw wide at 21:9 or wider, and only its top 55–60svh is visible before the fold, the rest arriving with scroll. The heading therefore reads as a large caption to the picture. On an object-led page the image is the object on a flat dark panel inset by the page margin instead of bleeding to the edges.
On a phone: Heading at 12vw on two lines, left-aligned, taking the top 30% of the screen. The two captions merge into one four-line block at 14px. The photograph begins at 45% of the screen height in a 4:5 crop, full-bleed, and runs off the bottom.

### 2. showcase — Band of unequal panels (strip)
A modest heading, then one wide band of pictures. The heading, two to four words at 36–44px, starts at column 3, left of centre, with 64px beneath it. The band spans the full viewport width edge to edge and is 68svh tall: four photographs standing side by side with no gaps and deliberately unequal widths, a flex row with flex-grow values of 5, 2, 3 and 2. Each panel is cropped with object-fit: cover to a tall slice, so the band reads as one folded view. The first panel carries a two-line title at 28px in paper colour at its top-left, 24px in, over a neutral scrim that covers only that corner. Hovering a panel raises its flex-grow to 6 over 500ms while the others give way; without hover the widths simply stay. One caption line at 16px sits under the band at the left margin.
On a phone: The heading sits on the margin at 28px. The band becomes a two-by-two block with no gaps, 84svh tall in total: the first row split 64/36 and the second 36/64, so the slices stay unequal. The title stays on the first panel. No hover behaviour.

### 3. features — One scene, stepping statements (full-bleed)
A wrapper 350–450vh tall holds a sticky full-bleed photograph (100vw by 100svh) with a calm, symmetrical subject and a clear upper third; a neutral dark scrim covers only that upper third. At top centre, five or six short statements of two to five words are stacked at 18–20px with 10px between them; the first of them is the section's heading. One statement is at full white and the rest at 45% opacity; the active line steps down the stack as the reader scrolls (IntersectionObserver on invisible 70vh markers). For each statement, two small photographs with square corners, 160–240px wide and of different ratios, fade in left and right of the centre line at different heights, then leave when the next pair arrives. No other text. A low-key image on a deep ground works equally well.
On a phone: The photograph stays sticky at 100svh, cropped to its centre. The statement stack moves to the bottom third over a scrim, at 17px, left-aligned on the margin. Each statement brings one small photograph only, 44vw wide, placed in the top-right corner with a 16px margin.

Adjusted for this page:
- It fades text: anything a visitor reads, inactive items of a list included, is `--ink` or `--muted` at full strength (contrast 4.5:1 or more). The active item is told apart by `--ink` against `--muted`, weight or the accent, not by fading the rest.
- It names colours (white): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).

### 4. showcase — Frame run, one lifted (strip)
Columns 1–3 are a nearly empty column: the heading (two to four words at 24–28px) at the top, a link under it, and one or two short notes at 15px much further down, each level with the frame it describes. Columns 4–12 hold a run of six to eight frames of the same subject at one fixed height of 30vh, with natural widths and 6px gaps: the whole view, a tight crop of the same thing, a detail, the reverse side. The run is allowed to leave the right edge. One frame is lifted out of the row: shown at 2.2 times the row height with its bottom aligned to the row's bottom (flex row, align-items: end), so it stands above the others. Under each frame, at 15px: a name on the left and a single fact on the right. Square corners, no captions on the images.
On a phone: The heading and link sit on top. The lifted frame comes first at full width. The rest of the run scrolls sideways beneath it at a fixed height of 34vw with 4px gaps and scroll-snap, bleeding off the right edge. Notes are listed under the run at 15px.

### 5. cta — Last photograph, text beside (full-bleed)
A final full-bleed photograph, 100vw by 90svh, chosen so its subject sits in the left half and its right half is calm. All text is confined to the right half and never crosses the centre line: in columns 8–12, anchored to the lower third, a closing line of three to five words at about 3.4vw in a light weight, a paragraph of two or three lines at 17px, and one filled button in the paper colour with ink text, about 52px tall. If the image behind the text is not calm enough, darken the whole photograph slightly with `filter: brightness(0.8)` rather than laying a panel behind the words. The section butts directly against a footer in one solid colour, so the photograph's bottom edge is a hard line. No logo, no second action.
On a phone: The photograph is 100vw by 70svh, cropped to keep its subject. The text leaves the image and sits beneath it on the footer's solid colour: closing line at 28px, paragraph at 17px, full-width button. Image and colour meet at a hard edge.

### 7. contact — Sentence, portrait, two links (band)
One closing row of three elements hanging from the same top line, with open ground between them, on a 12-column grid. Columns 1–5: a sentence of 12–18 words at clamp(26px, 3vw, 44px), four lines, leading 1.1, saying what kind of enquiries are welcome; it is set as a paragraph and there is no separate heading. Columns 8–9: one small square portrait or photograph of the place, 150–170px, with square corners. Columns 10–12: a short question at 18px in medium weight, then 20px below it two underlined links stacked at 18px (the email address and one booking or social link), and beneath them a line at 14px in the soft ink with the city and the usual reply time. Columns 6–7 stay empty. The row has 160px of space above and 120px below. No form. On a dark ground the photograph is the only bright thing in the row.

## 8. Motion — Shutter image
The one hero or showcase image opens from an edge with a clip-path over about 1.1 seconds while the picture inside settles from 1.12 scale to rest. Use it once, on the image that matters most; later images simply are there. Recipe: 'Image shutter' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Placement. The differing sizes and offsets create movement on a still page.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Uneven is not random: every image edge lands on a column line, and offsets come from a small set of values. On a phone, keep the unevenness with alternating widths (100%, 72% left, 60% right) rather than stacking everything full width.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
