This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
Art-direct the subject as if it belonged to a different, more vivid category. A money app is sold like a streetwear drop. An industrial roaster is presented like a design object. Drilling hardware gets the calm of a watch brand. A parcel-locker service becomes a fairground of stickers. Choose the borrowed category by asking what feeling the audience is missing from this kind of product (swagger, calm, glamour, play) and which category owns that feeling. Then take its manners completely: its type, its pacing, its way of photographing things. The contrast between dull subject and vivid dress is what people remember.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Depends on the borrowed category, so decide it explicitly and write it in your notes. Fashion drop: one shouted condensed headline across the full width, a single cropped image under it, then product facts as a blunt list. Watch or perfume: a centred object on a dark field, a small serif name, very few words, long vertical pauses. Poster or zine: flat colour blocks, heavy type, tilted cut-out images. Museum: one item per screen on a plinth-like band of colour with a short label beside it. Whichever you choose, the page structure stays simple (four to six sections) and every section uses the borrowed manners, including pricing and the footer.

## 3. Type — Boska
A high-contrast serif with pointed serifs and a lively italic, used roman and italic together at one size. Suits perfume, jewellery, lodges, weddings and other occasions for ceremony.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=boska@400,401,500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Boska** — weight 400 · tracking -0.02em · leading 0.95 · roman capitals for the main words with lowercase italic for the connecting words at the same size, or sentence case throughout; centred lines work
- Text: **Boska** — 400 for body, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.5 · tracking 0.005em
```css

  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
The borrowed category's type, fully committed: condensed and loud, or refined and small. No halfway.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 6. Hero — Counter object, wide wordmark
Borrowed from a watch or perfume counter. One flat charcoal field. Centred slightly above the midline, a single low-key photograph of the object about 31% of the viewport wide (columns 5-8), 4:5, its own dark background matched to the field with filter: brightness(0.9) so it sits without a frame. Under it the name runs on one line as an ultra-wide wordmark spanning 72% of the width yet only about 45px tall: an extended face with letter-spacing near 0.6em, or a regular face stretched with transform: scaleX(2.4). Then one centred serif sentence at clamp(24px, 2.6vw, 38px). Then a single centred row at 15px with 48px between its three parts: one specification, the price inside the one solid light rectangle that is also the action, a second specification. The wordmark repeats small top-left and one text link sits top-right, both on 32px margins. The bottom fifth and both outer quarters of the screen stay empty and dark.

On a phone: The photograph grows to 70% of the width at 4:5, its top at 16% of the height. The wide wordmark spans the full width inside 20px margins at about 28px tall. The serif sentence sets at 6vw on two lines. The row stacks into three centred lines with the action in the middle at full width. The lowest sixth of the screen stays dark and empty.
Headline: The name as a very wide, very low wordmark on one line (up to 12 characters); the serif sentence beneath is the supporting line, up to 40 characters.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Boska, text Boska) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.
- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. intro — Staircase statement (statement)
On a flat dark or saturated field one sentence of 12–18 words is broken into five short lines at clamp(36px, 4.4vw, 64px), bold, leading 1.02, marked up as one paragraph (a statement, so it may run past two lines at this size). Each line starts further right than the one above: indents of 0, 6, 12, 18 and 24vw from a 4vw left margin, set with `padding-left` per line, so the block descends like stairs and ends near the 75% mark. Each line takes its own flat colour from the palette, or lines alternate between two inks. The block is vertically centred in a section 100svh tall. The right quarter (columns 10–12) holds two stacked photographs, each 3:2 and 20vw wide with a 16px gap, top-aligned with the first line. No heading; a two-line note at 15px sits at the bottom-left margin, on the first line's left edge. On a page without images, leave the right quarter empty.
On a phone: Five lines at 9vw (35px on a 390px screen) with indents of 0, 5, 10, 15 and 20vw, so the stair still reads on a 390px screen. The two photographs follow below as one row of two equal 3:2 frames. The note sits last, left-aligned.

Adjusted for this page:
- It numbers things 01, 02: drop the numbers unless they are real data (a price, a date, a count).

### 2. showcase — Output slips along the edge (full-bleed)
For subjects whose result is a piece of text or paper: a receipt, a note, a ticket, a written line. A full-bleed photograph, 100vw by 96svh, sets the scene. In the upper centre, left-aligned in a column starting at 18% of the width, two short paragraphs in paper colour at 28–32px, the first opening with the heading's two to four words; a neutral scrim sits behind that column only. Along the bottom, six paper-coloured slips stand side by side across the full width with 12px gaps, each a filled rectangle of a different height (between 26 and 44svh), their lower ends cut off by the section's bottom edge. Two or three of the slips, not all, are tilted by 1–2 degrees. Each slip carries a sample of real output in the text face at 15px: a bold first line, a divider made of typed characters, then four to eight lines.
On a phone: The photograph is 100svh. The paragraphs sit in the top third at 22px. Only three slips show, each 30vw wide, standing along the bottom at different heights, and the row scrolls sideways to reveal the rest. Slip text stays at 14px; do not scale it down.

Adjusted for this page:
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.

### 3. features — Capabilities as a menu (table)
The capabilities are set as a printed menu or price list. One paper-coloured sheet, 520–600px wide and taller than it is wide, sits on a contrasting flat ground, centred or starting at column 2; its top and bottom edges are cut into shallow scallops or a zigzag with clip-path, its sides straight. On it, centred at the top, the heading: two or three words at 44–56px, then 40px of space. Below, items in two or three groups; each group opens with its name at 20px bold. Each item is a row: the name at 19px on the left, a short value on the right (a price, a time, a quantity, 'included'), and between them a leader of round 4px dots (border-bottom: 4px dotted, sitting on the baseline). A description at 15px in the soft ink sits under the name, at most 34ch. 18px between items, 44px between groups.
On a phone: The sheet fills the width minus 12px margins and keeps its cut top and bottom edges. Names stay left and values right on one line with the dot leader between; descriptions wrap beneath at 15px. Group names drop to 18px. Padding inside the sheet is 20px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.

### 4. showcase — Close-up with column card (full-bleed)
A full-bleed close-up photograph, 100vw by 100svh, with nothing over most of it. A four-column grid lies on top. One solid card in the ink colour, exactly one grid column wide (about 22vw) and 52svh tall, sits in the second column, vertically centred. Inside it, centred: the item's name at 30–36px, a variant line at 17px, the price or key fact at 17px, a small 3:2 photograph of the whole object (the card shows the whole, the background shows the detail), and at the foot a square-cornered action filling the card's width. The card is opaque, radius 0–6px, with no blur. When the section presents two or three items, each gets its own full-bleed frame and its card moves one column to the right each time, so position carries the sequence. The first card's name is the section heading.
On a phone: The photograph fills 100svh, cropped to its most detailed area. The card becomes a full-width block docked to the bottom, 46svh tall, left-aligned inside with 20px padding; the small whole-object photograph sits to the right of the name at 96px wide.

### 5. cta — One word is the button (band)
The entire band is one link. A full-bleed field in the accent colour, about 60svh tall, contains a single verb of four to eight letters set at 24–30vw in the heaviest weight, tight tracking, sized with `clamp()` so it spans the content width on one line with its baseline 24px above the bottom edge. The `<a>` wraps the whole band; on hover and focus the field and the word swap colours instantly. In the top-left corner, at 20px, one sentence says what happens next; in the top-right, an arrow drawn as a simple inline SVG, 48px, points up and right. There is no other button anywhere in the section. In a page with a motif, the band's top corners take the motif's shape (a large radius, or a chamfer cut with `clip-path`). Keep the word honest: the action itself, not a slogan.
On a phone: The band is 46svh tall. The word is set at whatever size fills the width on one line; choose a shorter verb rather than going below 22vw. Sentence top-left at 17px across two lines, arrow top-right at 32px. The whole band stays tappable.

### 6. about — Two columns, highlighted words (statement)
The story is set as text far larger than anything else on the page: 110–160 words at clamp(22px, 2.1vw, 32px), tight leading 1.18, in the soft ink, flowing through two columns that fill the full width between 24px margins (`columns: 2; column-gap: 32px`, justified, with hyphenation on). No heading. The name at the start is in full ink. Six or seven single words or short phrases carry a flat highlighter background in three or four palette colours, built with an inline `background-color`, `box-decoration-break: clone` and 0.1em of horizontal padding; they mark the facts a scanning reader wants (a place, a year, a client, a skill). The final short sentence is highlighted entire in the strongest colour and is the link to contact. Padding 120px top and bottom. No image and no portrait.
On a phone: One column at 20px, left-aligned and unjustified, leading 1.25, with 18px margins. Keep four or five highlights so no line carries two. The final highlighted sentence sits on its own line with 24px above it and works as a full-width tap target.

### 7. contact — Clip-out coupon form (split)
The form is a coupon to cut out. One filled card in a strong flat colour, about 2.2:1, spans columns 2–11 with a 14px radius, with 120px of ground above and below. Semicircular notches 28px across are cut into its top and bottom edges at the 46% mark with `clip-path` or an SVG `mask`, and a vertical row of round dots (a dotted `border-left` 5px thick in the ground colour) joins the two notches as the perforation. Left of the perforation, with 48px padding: a two-line heading of three to five words at clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px) in heavy capitals at the top, and one sentence at 16px at the bottom. Right of it: four inputs stacked with 10px gaps, each a filled block 52px tall in a lighter tone of the card with its placeholder at 16px, then a full-width solid submit bar 56px tall in the darkest ink. Inputs keep visually hidden `label` elements.
On a phone: The card turns upright and spans the width with 12px margins. The perforation becomes horizontal: notches on the left and right edges, dots across. Heading and sentence sit above it; the four inputs and the submit bar sit below, each 54px tall.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names its own corners: use the one radius of this page (square unless the direction says otherwise) and one button shape.
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Boska, text Boska) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.

### 8. footer — Contact as the headline (statement)
A full-bleed field in one saturated palette colour, at least 90svh tall. Top row, 40px from the edge: two or three short notes at 15px in columns 1–7 (address, hours, a one-line description) and a back-to-top text link at the right margin. Then a tall empty gap. In the lower half, the way to get in touch is the display line: the telephone number or the email address itself, set at 8–9vw, flush left, hard against the margin, as one link. An email address may break once, before the @, giving two lines. Size it with `clamp()` so the longest line fills at least 80% of the width. Under it, one row of small text at 14px spread across three positions: links at the left, credits in the centre, city and year at the right. The wordmark appears only small, in the top row.
On a phone: Notes stack at the top at 15px. The contact line sits in the lower half at 12vw; a telephone number stays on one line, an email breaks before the @ and is sized so each half fits the width. The meta row becomes three stacked lines at 13px.

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Attitude. The page has a recognisable voice from another world, applied to a subject nobody expected it for.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Name the borrowed category in one word before you start, or the result is merely loud. The content must still be clear: what it is, what it costs, how to get it. Avoid borrowing from 'tech startup', which is the default you are escaping.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
